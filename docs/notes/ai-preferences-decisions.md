# AI Assistance and Guidance Preferences — Decisions

Updated: October 17, 2026

Status: The four preference controls and optional adjustment behavior are agreed. Defaults and final presentation remain to be specified. No app development has started. Synced project sources remain read-only.

## Confirmed context

- Common workflow for novice and experienced instructional designers, with adjustable guidance.
- AI creates editable starting points from reviewed previous-stage records. Designer decisions govern acceptance and handoff.
- Optional reflection on AI outputs, level of contribution, output preferences, and additional resources. Reflection does not block progression.
- Optional pause/resume support, including review breaks and a return recap.
- The AI process is an agreed baseline to revisit with reviewed examples, prompt testing, and refinements during AI integration in each phase.

## Agreed controls

1. Guidance depth: brief hints or step-by-step explanations/examples. This controls help for the designer, independently of drafting contribution.
2. AI contribution: limited new suggestions or fuller draft proposals. Both retain the full structured skeleton, carried-forward reviewed content, and visible gaps. Neither level authorizes AI acceptance of its own work.
3. Output detail: concise, standard, or detailed wording/build-note explanations. Essential content, assessment definitions, source references, accessibility requirements, and required build specifications remain present at every level.
4. Additional learning resources: essential guidance or deeper instructional-design resources on request. Project-source evidence remains accessible regardless of this setting; resources are linked/verified under the shared source rules.

Apply preferences at project level and permit changes at any time. Defaults have not yet been selected. Explain what each setting changes using short examples rather than model/provider jargon.

After a stage review, optionally offer “Adjust AI assistance” with skip/continue. Do not force a questionnaire or repeatedly interrupt designers with prompts. These controls remain accessible without completing reflection.

Changes affect future generation. Applying changed preferences to existing content requires an explicit scoped revision request and preserves accepted content until the designer approves proposed changes. Presentation preferences cannot override evidence rules, scope, reviewed decisions, Bloom alignment, or quality requirements.

The user approved the four controls, their stated options/purposes, project-level adjustment, optional reflection, preservation of essential content, and explicit revision of existing work. Default selections and final UI remain to be specified.

## Pause and resume — agreed detailed behavior

The user approved the feature and the behavior below. Detailed visual presentation remains future work.

- “Save and pause” is available throughout Analysis, Design, and partial Development. Preserve current answers, edits, selected section, and review status. Autosave continues to support ordinary work as separately agreed.
- Break reminders are off by default; designers can enable and adjust them, dismiss a reminder, or turn them off. No forced breaks or countdowns.
- On pause, offer a quiet pause or an optional short break activity. Activities must include choices that do not require movement, vision, or sound, and must not become mandatory work.
- On return, show a short resume card identifying the prior stage/section, last saved action, unresolved issues relevant to that location, and a suggested next step. Resume at the saved location. Use saved project state for routine recap rather than requiring a new AI call on every return.
- Pausing/resuming or enabling reminders must not regenerate project content, change accepted decisions, or alter progression gates. Show any in-progress generation status clearly and preserve its result when complete; pausing the UI must not falsely imply a running generation was canceled.
- Provide direct keyboard access and controllable/dismissible prompts. No timed response is required to continue work.

The user approved this pause/resume behavior, including optional reminders off by default and a resume recap, and raised automatic saving as a required protection against losing work.
