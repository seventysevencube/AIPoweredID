## Section 2: Evidence and possible causes — agreed

1. What information supports your description of the performance gap? Examples: observations, work samples, stakeholder feedback, errors, or performance measures. Allow “Evidence not yet available.”
2. Add documentation of any supporting evidence if available

   Optional pasted text or Word (.docx)/PowerPoint (.pptx) uploads with basic extraction; no scanned-document extraction. Preserve source references.
3. What might be contributing to the gap? Select any that may apply: knowledge or skills; unclear expectations; process or workflow; tools or access; time or resources; incentives or competing priorities; other; not sure. These are hypotheses, not established causes.
4. What has already been tried, and what happened? Allow “Nothing yet” and “Not known.”

AI should distinguish evidence from hypotheses, flag unanswered questions, and avoid treating the requested training as proof that training is the right intervention. The user approved Section 2 with the revised supporting-material wording above.

## Section 5: Evaluation planning — agreed

Confirmed scope clarification: the user wants full evaluation-planning questions in Analysis to identify Kirkpatrick L2, L3, and L4 measures. Capturing and carrying forward the plan is in scope; administering evaluation, collecting results, analyzing results, and operating an Evaluation module remain outside the MVP. This clarification supersedes the earlier proposal for a limited success-indicator section.

Agreed sequence: L4 results → L3 workplace behavior → L2 learning. Reuse relevant earlier answers and supporting documentation; do not require duplicate entry. The user approved the questions and per-measure details below.

### Level 4 — Results

1. What organizational or business result should this project contribute to? Prefill relevant earlier desired-performance and reason-for-request responses.
2. Which indicators would show progress toward that result, including early indicators and longer-term outcomes where relevant?
3. What current baseline and target exist for each indicator? Allow unknown and distinguish proposed targets from agreed targets.
4. What other changes or factors could affect these results, and how could their influence be considered when interpreting findings? Do not assume a change in results proves training caused it.

### Level 3 — Behavior

1. What specific actions should people perform on the job to contribute to those results?
2. What would correct and consistent performance look like, and what evidence could show it is happening?
3. What support, reinforcement, or accountability will help sustain those actions?
4. What workplace barriers or lack of opportunity could prevent people from applying what they learn?

### Level 2 — Learning

1. What knowledge and skills must people gain to perform those workplace actions? Where relevant, what attitudes, confidence, or commitment should also be measured?
2. What evidence would demonstrate that learning, such as a scenario response, demonstration, work product, or knowledge check?
3. How will learning or change be established—for example, a baseline comparison or a defined performance standard?
4. What criteria would indicate successful learning? Self-reported confidence or commitment must not be treated as proof of knowledge or skill.

Analysis identifies intended learning outcomes, candidate measures, and criteria. Objectives and detailed assessment questions/tasks, answers/rubrics, feedback, and scoring remain defined and reviewed during Design, then carried into the storyboard. Do not generate final assessment items in Analysis.

### Details captured for each selected measure

- Linked level, intended result/behavior/learning outcome, and a stable measure ID.
- Measure definition, baseline, target/criterion, and status (known, proposed, unknown, or not applicable with rationale).
- Data source, collection method, and feasibility of access; reference existing documents rather than reuploading.
- Who is responsible for collection and who reviews the findings.
- Baseline timing, follow-up timing, and collection frequency as appropriate to work opportunities; do not impose fixed dates such as 30/60/90 days.
- What decision or action the findings will inform.

These details could use repeatable measure cards to keep the form manageable; exact layout remains to be agreed. Under the subsequently agreed conditional gate, L2 measures can remain unresolved on entry to the initial Six Boxes review when applicability is unclear, but must be identified before instructional Design proceeds. They cannot remain “Not known yet” on a confirmed learning route. A route with no learning component records L2 as not applicable with a rationale. Other details may allow unknown or infeasibility as described below. AI must not invent values or claim that every project can establish impact at all levels.

### Handoff and AI behavior

Produce an editable evaluation-plan table and carry it into Design with links L4 result → L3 behavior → L2 learning evidence → Design objectives and assessments as these are developed. Carry proposed and unknown status forward. Flag conflicts and missing links for review, rather than silently resolving them. Include the reviewed plan in the project handoff/export; exact presentation remains to be agreed.

Reference checked: https://www.kirkpatrickpartners.com/the-kirkpatrick-model/ (official definitions, working backward from results, and methods). Section 5 questions and evaluation-planning approach are approved; exact presentation remains to be agreed.

## Required versus optional information — agreed

All five Analysis question sections and the following completion rules are agreed, including the user-required L2 exception.

- Saving is always allowed, including with incomplete fields.
- Core information for a useful Design draft: workplace task, desired performance, current performance, and initial audience description. A working project name can be supplied or generated for review.
- Supporting documentation and supplementary explanations are optional.
- Stakeholder availability, timelines, resources, constraints, L3/L4 measures, and other evaluation-plan details can remain “Not known yet.” L2 measure identification follows the conditional gate below. Relevant fields can use “Not applicable” with an explanation; a performance-support deliverable does not by itself determine learning applicability.
- Confirmed conditional L2 gate: allow entry to the first Design performance-solution review while L2 applicability is unresolved. Once a learning route is confirmed, L2 measures must be identified and designer-confirmed before instructional Design proceeds; an incomplete skeleton cannot bypass that requirement. If no learning component is selected, record “Not applicable — no learning intervention” with rationale. Designers can always save unfinished work. AI may propose measures but cannot approve them. Baseline, target, timing, and owner fields are not all made mandatory by this rule.
- L2 measure identification specifies what learning will be measured and the type of evidence/method that will demonstrate it. Detailed assessment items, answer keys/rubrics, feedback, and scoring remain developed and reviewed in Design.
- Before handoff, show a concise summary of gaps. Designers can resolve them or explicitly request an incomplete Design skeleton. Such a skeleton preserves gaps and cannot be presented as a complete, reviewed Design plan.
- Missing evidence or unconfirmed evaluation baselines/targets must never be silently invented by AI.
- Field-completion rules are distinct from designer acceptance of generated recommendations; accepting an incomplete handoff does not confirm its unknown facts.
