## Design opening: Performance analysis

### Part 1: Review the performance evidence

Prefill from the accepted Analysis brief: desired performance, current performance, audience, and supporting evidence. Designers review this information rather than enter it again.

1. **Does this describe the performance problem accurately?**
   Show an editable summary of what people need to do and what is happening now. Let the designer correct it or identify information that remains uncertain.
2. **Where in the work does the difficulty occur?**
   Identify the affected task, decision, or step. Reuse any details already captured in Analysis. “Not yet clear” remains available.
3. **What does the evidence show about when the difficulty occurs?**
   Capture relevant patterns—for example, differences between situations, available resources, or working conditions. Ask about successful performance where evidence is available, too. These are optional prompts, not required categories.
4. **What do we still need to verify before choosing a response?**
   List unanswered questions and possible explanations separately from observed findings. The designer can identify what additional evidence would help.

For optional supporting material, retain the exact prompt: **“Add documentation of any supporting evidence if available”**.

AI organizes the existing evidence, links findings to their sources, and highlights gaps or conflicting information. Any explanation it suggests is clearly marked as a hypothesis for designer review. Poor performance alone must not become a conclusion that someone lacks knowledge, ability, or motivation.

Output: a reviewed evidence summary and open questions. The following section uses those findings to consider possible explanations and confirm the response route.

### Part 2: Consider possible explanations and choose a response

The reviewed evidence carries forward into this section.

1. **What might explain the difficulty at the affected task or step?**
   AI proposes possible explanations linked to the evidence. The designer can edit, add, or remove them. Multiple explanations may apply; “Not enough information” is available.
2. **What supports or challenges each explanation?**
   Show the supporting observations, conflicting evidence, and unanswered questions alongside each explanation. Keep an explanation marked **Unverified** when the evidence does not establish it.
3. **Would knowing what to do and being able to do it be enough to achieve the expected performance?**
   Help the designer consider whether people would still encounter obstacles during the actual work. Allow **Yes**, **No**, or **Uncertain**, with an explanation. This answer informs the decision without automatically determining it.
4. **Based on the evidence, which response should this project pursue?**

   | Response | Meaning |
   |---|---|
   | **Learning** | Develop knowledge or skills needed for the task. |
   | **Changes beyond learning** | Address conditions preventing successful performance. |
   | **Both** | Develop knowledge or skills and address other contributing conditions. |
   | **Further investigation** | Gather more evidence before choosing a response. |

AI suggests a route with its reasoning and uncertainties. The designer explicitly confirms or changes it, recording the reason.

After confirmation:

- **Learning or both:** confirm the linked learning measures before proceeding with instructional Design. Detailed assessments come later.
- **Changes beyond learning:** record why learning measures are not applicable and retain relevant workplace-performance and organizational-results planning.
- **Further investigation:** preserve the draft and identify what needs checking; instructional drafting stays on hold.

Choosing e-learning, a performance aid, or both remains a subsequent decision.

## Analysis: Evaluation planning

Existing Analysis information is reused. This section plans evaluation; collecting or analyzing results remains outside the MVP.

### Part 1: Organizational results

This part connects the performance problem to a wider organizational outcome. Existing Analysis information is prefilled.

1. **What organizational result should improved performance contribute to?**
   Describe the intended benefit, such as fewer avoidable errors, reduced rework, or improved service quality. “Not yet known” is available.
2. **What information would help you judge whether that result is improving?**
   Identify one or more indicators. Reuse relevant measures already captured rather than creating duplicates.
3. **For each indicator, what is the current position and the desired result?**
   Record the baseline and target where known, including their source. Allow either to remain unknown; AI must not invent figures.
4. **How would each indicator be checked?**
   Capture the data source and collection method; person or role responsible; when and how often it would be checked; and how findings would inform decisions.
5. **What else could influence this result?**
   Record other changes or conditions that could affect the indicator. Improvement alone should not be treated as proof that the learning or support caused it.

AI can suggest indicators and highlight missing details, but suggestions remain proposals until reviewed. Unknowns can carry forward without blocking Design.

### Part 2: Workplace performance

Plan how to determine whether people perform the actual work successfully. Reuse the desired performance and evidence already captured in Analysis.

1. **What should people do differently or more consistently during their work?**
   Prefill the relevant tasks or decisions for review. Describe observable performance rather than broad intentions such as “be more confident.”
2. **What would show that they are performing successfully?**
   Identify evidence such as work quality, correct decisions, fewer errors, or appropriate use of a procedure. Include the conditions and success criteria where known.
3. **What is happening now, and what improvement is expected?**
   Record the baseline, desired performance, and supporting source for each measure. Unknowns are allowed; AI must not invent values.
4. **How would each measure be checked?**
   Capture evidence source and collection method; person or role responsible; timing and frequency, allowing an opportunity to apply the performance at work; and how findings would inform decisions.
5. **What could affect people’s opportunity to demonstrate this performance?**
   Record relevant working conditions or dependencies. Distinguish **not performing successfully** from **not having an opportunity to perform**.

Where relevant, link these measures to the organizational results already identified. These measures apply to learning and nonlearning responses, and permitted unknowns can carry forward.

### Part 3: Evidence of learning

Identify what would demonstrate that people have developed the knowledge or skills needed for the work. This establishes the measurement intent. Detailed objectives and assessments are developed later in Design.

1. **What knowledge or skills would people need to develop to perform the work successfully?**
   Reuse the performance gap and evidence already captured. Link each identified need to the relevant workplace task or decision.
2. **What would people need to demonstrate to show they have learned it?**
   Describe observable evidence—for example, making an appropriate decision, explaining a choice, or completing a task. Attendance or course completion alone does not demonstrate learning.
3. **What would count as a successful demonstration?**
   Identify the expected accuracy, quality, independence, or other relevant criteria, including any resources people may use. Record current capability and the desired improvement where known; AI must not invent baselines or targets.
4. **How would evidence of learning be gathered and used?**
   For each measure, capture proposed evidence source and collection method; person or role responsible; timing and frequency; and how findings would guide feedback, additional support, or revisions. Describe the approach here; exact assessment tasks, answers, scoring, and feedback come later.

During Analysis, the learning need may remain unresolved while the response route is undecided.

At the beginning of Design:

- **Learning or combined route:** the designer must review and explicitly confirm the defined learning measures before instructional Design proceeds.
- **Nonlearning route:** record why learning measures are not applicable.
- **Further investigation:** retain unanswered questions and hold instructional drafting.

Confirmed measures carry into objective and assessment planning. Later refinements remain explicit and preserve their links.

## Performance-analysis desktop layout — approved

The user approved the following two-step desktop layout:

1. **Review evidence:** prefilled findings in the main area, with supporting sources available beside them.
2. **Consider explanations and choose a response:** each explanation shows supporting evidence, conflicting evidence, and unknowns. The response choice follows underneath.

Keep AI suggestions visibly marked as proposals, with a separate action to confirm the response route. Show learning-measure confirmation afterward only when applicable under the approved route rules.

This is approval of the presentation approach, not a rendered prototype or implemented application.

## Evaluation planning desktop layout — approved

Within Analysis, use one workspace with three sections in the left navigation: **Organizational results**, **Workplace performance**, and **Evidence of learning**.

Each section shows the approved questions and prefills relevant Analysis information. Each measure gets an editable card containing its description, baseline and target, evidence source, collection method, responsibility, timing, and intended use. Preserve question-specific fields such as other influences, opportunity to perform, demonstration conditions, and success criteria.

- Expand one measure at a time, with clear summaries for the others.
- Keep missing information visible as **Not yet known**.
- Show links between related measures.
- Offer guidance beside the relevant question.
- Label AI suggestions as proposals for review.

Designers can save and review incomplete plans. Learning-measure confirmation remains a separate step at the beginning of Design, once a learning or combined route is confirmed. This layout does not create a bypass of that gate.
