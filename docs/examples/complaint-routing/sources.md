## SOP-01 — Complaint-routing guide, exercise version 1

### SOP-01 §1 — Scope and responsibilities

The exercise covers two complaint types: routine order-status questions and reported damaged items. Representatives use the guide while handling cases. Other complaint types and ambiguous cases beyond these rules go to the team lead for direction; do not invent a route or promise an outcome.

### SOP-01 §2 — Relevant information

Review the order reference, the customer's description of the concern, and details needed to distinguish the two supported complaint types. Determine whether the customer is asking about delivery timing or reporting an item as damaged. Do not assume that a delivery question includes damage, or that damage is absent when the description is unclear.

If information needed to apply these rules is missing or ambiguous, ask for clarification and record what is still needed. Do not make a final routing decision until the relevant distinction can be made; consult the team lead if uncertainty persists.

### SOP-01 §3 — Rule 1: Routine order-status question

When the customer asks when an order will arrive and no damage is reported after any needed clarification, the representative handles the order-status question. It is not escalated solely because the customer asks about delivery timing.

### SOP-01 §4 — Rule 2: Damaged-item complaint

When the customer reports an item as damaged, route the complaint to the resolution team. Record the detail that makes this rule applicable. The exercise does not authorize representatives to promise a refund, replacement, or resolution deadline.

### SOP-01 §5 — Record the decision

For either supported complaint type, record the relevant case information, category, selected response/route, and a short explanation tied to the applicable rule. Identify unresolved information rather than filling it with an assumption.

## EVID-01 — Fictional case audit

Twenty relevant cases were reviewed using the same two-category exercise guide. These are case counts, not counts of individual employees. The sample is illustrative and not claimed to represent all work.

| Case type | Cases reviewed | Correct initial decision | Incorrect initial decision |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Routine order-status questions | 12 | 8 handled by representative | 4 unnecessarily escalated |
| Reported damaged items | 8 | 4 routed to resolution team | 4 retained by representative |
| Total | 20 | 12 | 8 |

Correct initial routing in this sample is 12/20 (60%). The audit alone does not establish why the errors occurred, how many cases were subsequently returned for correction, or the effect on customer outcomes.

## EVID-02 — Fictional observations and interviews

- Six representatives participated in a guided observation, separate from the twenty-case audit. No individual participant is linked to an audit error.
- Four of the six could not locate the current routing guide through their usual reference location without assistance.
- After the current guide was provided, four applied both supported routing rules correctly and explained their choices; two still confused the two routes.
- These limited observations suggest questions about resource access and application of the rules. They do not prove a general skills deficit, lack of effort, motivation, or capacity problem.
- No systematic evidence about incentives, workload pressure, or assignment suitability has been collected.
- A team reminder had previously restated the two categories, but no before/after evidence was gathered. Its effect is unknown.

They work on desktop computers and can use approved digital reference material during the task. Written English is the initial language; no translated version has been commissioned.
